

# CW (Morse Code)

[CW (Continuous Wave)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_wave) is one of the oldest and simplest forms of communication used in amateur (ham) radio. It uses [Morse code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_code) to transmit information through a series of short and long signals, known as dits (dots) and dahs (dashes). Despite its age, CW remains a popular operating mode among radio amateurs worldwide, valued for its simplicity, efficiency, and unique operating experience.

## How CW Works

CW works by switching a radio transmitter's carrier signal on and off in a controlled pattern. When the transmitter is keyed, it sends a continuous radio-frequency signal; when the key is released, the signal stops. The duration and spacing of these transmissions form Morse code characters.

A short transmission represents a dit (`·`), while a longer transmission represents a dah (`−`). Different combinations represent letters, numbers, and punctuation. For example, the letter A is `·−`, while SOS is `··· −−− ···`.

On the receiving side, the radio converts these signal pulses into audible tones, allowing the operator to recognize the Morse code by listening. The pitch of the tone is determined by the receiver, not by the transmitted signal.

CW can be sent using a traditional straight key, a paddle with an electronic keyer, or computer-generated Morse code.
